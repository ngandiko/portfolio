01

Overview

Tiny Threads is a peer-to-peer marketplace for pre-loved children's clothing. The design had to solve two problems at once: make listing low-value clothes feel worth the effort for sellers, and make buying them feel safe for parents.

  • Less effort to list

    AI-assisted listing turns photos and a few taps into a complete, honest listing.

  • Confidence to buy

    Condition, fit and flaws are visible before checkout — not buried in a description.

  • Built for both sides

    Seller efficiency and buyer trust were treated as one system, not competing goals.

02

The problem

Parents are stuck between waste and work. Children outgrow clothes in months, but reselling a $12 dress can take longer than it's worth: photographing, pricing, answering questions, shipping. Buyers face the mirror problem — uncertain about fit, condition and whether second-hand is actually cheaper once fees are added.

“My daughter outgrows her clothes so fast. I don't want them to go to waste, but it's hard to find an easy platform to sell without a lot of work.”

User interview · Tiny Threads research
03

Research

01

Interviews & surveys

Parents across age groups, family sizes and buying habits.

02

Secondary research

Studies, articles and reports on how families manage outgrown clothing.

03

10 platforms analysed

Poshmark, Vinted, Mercari, eBay, Depop, ThredUp, Once Upon a Child, OfferUp, Kidizen and Facebook Marketplace.

What I heard

  • 01

    Sellers weigh effort against return. For low-value items, the effort often wins.

  • 02

    Buyers need condition, fit and flaws before they will commit.

  • 03

    Second-hand value is judged against buying new — after shipping and fees.

  • 04

    Parents shop by practical need: size, season and wardrobe gaps, not browsing.

04

Who I designed for

Three behavioural archetypes, built from the interviews rather than from demographics alone.

28 · Stay-at-home parent

Efficiency-Seeking Seller

“I want an easy way to sell or give away clothes my child no longer wears.”
  • 3-year-old son
  • Wants a simple, quick listing process
  • Prefers giving clothes a second life over discarding

35 · Couple, two children

Convenience-Seeking Parent

“We want a platform where we can both find and pass on clothes as our kids grow.”
  • Children aged 2 and 5
  • Looks for budget-friendly options for multiple kids
  • Interested in both buying and selling

32 · Working professional

Selective Value Buyer

“I want to find stylish, good-quality clothes for my daughter without spending too much.”
  • 5-year-old daughter
  • Looks for quality at an affordable price
  • Values easy search and filters
05

What the research changed

01

Clear condition labels

Use the same plain language everywhere: excellent, good, gently used.

02

Easy filtering

Size, gender, brand, condition and price — the five things parents actually filter by.

03

Reduce effort on both sides

Cut the time to list, and the time to find something worth buying.

04

Trust is built from detail

Clear photos, honest descriptions and consistent information do more than badges.

Final design

Selling

09

Learnings

01

Seller effort is the real price

For low-value items, how long a listing takes matters as much as what it earns.

02

Trust lives in the details

Condition, sizing and visible flaws did more for buyer confidence than any trust badge.

03

Balance both sides

Great marketplace UX holds seller efficiency and buyer confidence in the same frame.
